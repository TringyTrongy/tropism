What correct looks like

  1. Dots. At zoom 1 the 5000 particles of Bodies read as separate, crisp dots; where bodies overlap, light paper darkens like ink and the dark print brightens.
  2. Seam. In Lattice, rows and columns stay straight and evenly spaced as they cross every dish edge, at any pan and zoom; no column doubles or goes missing.
  3. Markers. Specimen opens at zoom 8 centred on the corner of the world, so both seams run through it. Each column shows its own marker (circle, square, triangle, diamond, down-triangle, plus, cross, star) with clean 1 px edges and nothing cut at the seams. The bottom row overlaps neighbouring species to show the blend.
  4. Highlight. Every species except the chosen one drops to about a fifth of its ink.
  5. Trails. Moving bodies leave short streaks. Pause motion and the streaks fade completely back to paper, with no grey haze left behind. Panning or zooming resets them.
  6. Path. The tracked particle carries a hairline that fades with age and a 7 px ring at its head. The line is continuous where it crosses the world’s own seam inside a panned view, and breaks only where it leaves one edge of the dish and re-enters at the opposite edge.
  7. Pointer. The crosshair sits exactly under the cursor at every pan and zoom, and the round trip reads 0.000 px.
  8. Context. Lose context, then restore: drawing resumes with markers, trails and path intact. The Canvas2D renderer draws the same scenes, with squares in place of markers below a 4.2 px radius.
